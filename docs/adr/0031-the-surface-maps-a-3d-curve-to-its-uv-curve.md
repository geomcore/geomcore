# The surface maps a 3D curve to its UV curve, and curves do not depend on surfaces

`ParametricSurface::curve_3d_to_uv(&self, curve: &Curve, tol) -> Result<Curve2D, Curve3DToUvError>` gives the exact UV curve `c` of a 3D curve `C` lying on the surface `S`, in the same parameter: `S∘c = C`, so `self.point_at(c(t))` is `curve.point_at(t)` for every `t`. It is the mirror of `uv_curve_to_3d` (ADR 0030), and it replaces the six inherent `parametrize_on` methods of `Line`, `Circle`, `Ellipse`, `Parabola`, `Hyperbola` and `BSplineCurve`. `Curve3DToUvError` replaces `ParametrizeError`, with the same variants: `NotAnalytic` and `CurveNotOnSurface`. The free sampled fallback `parametrize_numeric` becomes `curve_3d_to_uv_numeric`, re-exported at the crate root next to `intersect_curve_surface`.

The method has the same shape as `uv_curve_to_3d`. It is a provided method whose default refuses with `NotAnalytic`, so a surface type defined outside the crate keeps compiling. Each crate surface overrides it with a one-line forward to `surfaces/uv_curve/from_curve.rs`, and `Surface` and `ElementarySurface` forward through their variant lists (ADR 0017). It takes `&Curve`, so the trait stays dyn-compatible.

**The closed forms live on the elementary surfaces.** The table is unchanged: a line or circle on the plane, cylinder, cone, sphere and torus where its image is a 2D line or circle, and every conic or spline on a plane. The forward reads the surface through `as_elementary`, and every curve on an extrusion, revolution, B-spline or Bézier surface is `NotAnalytic`, as it was. `curve_3d_to_uv_numeric` samples those pairs. Mapping a curve onto the extrusion of a line, the mirror of ADR 0030's affine rows, has no consumer yet and is left out.

**Taking `&Curve` adds `Curve::Bezier`.** A Bézier curve in a plane maps its poles exactly as a B-spline does, since both are the same spline underneath. Its image is a `BezierCurve2D` with the same weights, and a pole off the plane gives `CurveNotOnSurface`. On every other surface it is `NotAnalytic`, as a B-spline is.

**Curves do not depend on surfaces.** With `parametrize_on` gone, no production file under `src/curves/` names `crate::surfaces`, or any name that `src/lib.rs` re-exports from `surfaces`, as a whole word. The second ban closes the way back through the root re-exports, such as `use crate::Plane;`. `tests/module_dependencies.rs` reads the sources and fails on either. It builds the name list by parsing the `pub use surfaces::` statements in `src/lib.rs`, so a new or renamed surface type is covered without editing the test. Test code, meaning `tests.rs` files and `tests/` directories, may still build surfaces.

## Considered options

- **Keep `parametrize_on` as a deprecated shim**: rejected. The crate is pre-1.0, and the shim would keep the curves importing surfaces until it is removed. The migration is one line, given in the changelog.
- **A free function `curve_3d_to_uv(curve, surface, tol)`**: rejected. The surface owns both directions, as it owns `point_at` and `parameters_of` at point level.
- **Closed forms on the swept surfaces now**: deferred until a consumer needs them. The numeric fallback answers in the meantime.

## Consequences

Rust callers write `surface.curve_3d_to_uv(&circle.into(), tol)` for `circle.parametrize_on(surface, tol)`, and match on `Curve3DToUvError`. A caller holding a `Curve` no longer dispatches on its variant. Python moves the method from the 3D curve classes to every surface class: `surface.curve_3d_to_uv(curve, tol=None)` accepts any 3D curve class, raises `Curve3DToUvError`, and `geomcore.parametrize_numeric` becomes `geomcore.curve_3d_to_uv_numeric`. The stub-only `_ParametrizeOnMethods` class is gone. The method is now part of `_SurfaceMethods`.

Amended (#186): `curve_3d_to_uv_numeric` is replaced by `curve_3d_to_uv_within`, one UV curve within a deviation; see the amendment to ADR 0035.

Amended (#221): the swept surfaces now have closed forms for their iso-curves, since the plane cuts of swept surfaces (ADR 0023) are their consumer. On an extrusion, a generator line maps to a line along `v` and the profile translated along the direction to a line along `u`. On a revolution, a parallel circle maps to a line along `u` and the profile turned about the axis to a line along `v`. The forward reads the surface through `as_extrusion` and `as_revolution` when `as_elementary` finds no elementary surface. A translated or turned profile is recognized on samples against the profile at the same parameters, so it must run in the profile's own parameter, as the cuts build it. Every other curve on a swept surface stays `NotAnalytic`. The cuts themselves set the curve on the swept surface from the parameters they already hold: the profile parameter of each crossing, and the angle of each meridian. Inverting a generator line's origin on an ellipse profile cost five times the cut. `with_exact_uv_curves` then fills only the slots still empty, whatever the method, so a curve through crossings found numerically, such as a plane cutting the extrusion of a spline profile, gets the plane's curve too.
