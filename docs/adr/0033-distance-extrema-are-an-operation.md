# Distance extrema are an operation; projection belongs to the shape

The local minima of the distance between two curves lived inside the numeric intersection: the search in `intersect/numeric/curve_curve.rs`, its B-spline half in `curve_curve_hull.rs`, its results in `intersect/results/`. They now form their own operation, `operations/extrema/`, laid out like `discretization/` (ARCHITECTURE rule 3):

| File | Holds |
|---|---|
| `extrema/curve_curve.rs` | `curve_curve_extrema`, its seeds and refinement, `CurveCurveExtrema`, `CurveCurveExtremum` |
| `extrema/curve_curve_pieces.rs` | the minima search on the Bézier pieces of two B-splines |

**Point projection stays with each shape.** Projecting a point is asked for the point and its parameters; the distance comes with it. On every elementary shape it is the closed-form inversion and one evaluation: `parameters_of` then `point_at` on a plane, cylinder, cone, sphere or torus, `parameter_of` then `point_at` on a line or circle (the cone picks the nearer of its two generator lines in the meridian plane, the torus the nearer of its two tube circles, so the inversion of any point is its closest point). The inversion is core to the shape, used by intersections and UV curves, and `contains` is read from it. So `PointExtrema` (curves) and `project_point` (a required method of `ParametricSurface`) are implemented in the shape's file, as on surfaces; only their result types are shared, in `operations/projection.rs`. A first attempt moved the curve impls to `operations/`; it split each curve's inversion from its shape for no gain and left curves and surfaces organised differently, so it was undone.

**Extrema calls the shared Bézier-piece machinery, never the reverse.** The numeric curve–curve intersection is read from the minima at zero distance (`search_curve_curve`, `hits_at_minima`), so it calls `extrema::curve_curve`, and its own grid fallback is `sampled_curve_curve_extrema`, a named entry point rather than a flag on the search. The extrema search runs on machinery the intersection searches share and that stays in `intersect/numeric/`: the subdivision driver (ADR 0019), the Bézier pieces, a curve as a B-spline over a range with the map back (`pairs_with_bspline`), and the curve's parameter domain (`parameter_domain.rs`; its seeding for the minima search stays in extrema). The seeding of the curve-pair minima (`curve_curve_seeds`) lives in extrema, so that machinery never calls extrema. Two result types still cross: the intersection's candidate hits are reported as `CurveCurveExtremum`s, and the extrema carry the intersection's `Completeness` and `CurveCurveRegion`.

That machinery serves four searches (curve–curve hits and minima, curve–surface hits, the patch pairs that seed marching), not only the intersection. Moving it to a home of its own is left until curve–surface extrema need it too. The release build uses the default codegen units, so module layout changes what is inlined, and a split once cost 2.3 % on a benchmark: such a move is benchmarked first.

**Not done here.** Curve–surface and surface–surface extrema, and a local search refining one starting parameter next to the global one, have no caller yet. They go in `extrema/` when one appears (`curve_surface.rs`, `surface_surface.rs`).

The level-function extrema that seed marching are a different notion; their module is `marching/level_extrema.rs`, so that "extrema" in a path means distance extrema between shapes only.

## Amendment: one Python method for both extrema (#201)

In Python, a 3D curve's `extrema(other)` takes a point or a curve. A point gives its projections, a list of `CurveProjection`. A curve gives the `CurveCurveExtrema` of the pair, which `geomcore.curve_curve_extrema(first, second)` returned before; that function now warns and goes in the next release. Users tried `curve.extrema(other_curve)` first and got a type error about `Point`. The Rust split stays: `PointExtrema::extrema` with each shape, and the free function `curve_curve_extrema` in `operations/extrema/`. A 2D curve's `extrema` still takes a point alone, since there is no 2D curve-pair extrema.

The point case keeps its plain list. A list has `len` and iteration, and a point's projections have no completeness to report, so a result class would only wrap them. Point extrema on the elementary and swept surfaces, which only B-spline and Bézier surfaces have today, are a separate issue.

## Amendment: curve–surface and surface–surface extrema (#251)

A clearance check needs the distance between a curve and a surface and between two surfaces, so both pairs now have their extrema: `curve_surface_extrema` in `extrema/curve_surface.rs` and `surface_surface_extrema` in `extrema/surface_surface.rs`, with the numeric search they share in `extrema/surface_search.rs`. Their results, `CurveSurfaceExtrema` and `SurfaceSurfaceExtrema`, are built like `CurveCurveExtrema`: the minima closest first, each with the parameters on both shapes, both points and the distance, and the search's completeness. The minimum distance of a pair is the closest of them (`closest()`); no separate function computes it, since the search that proves a global minimum is the one that finds the local ones.

**Minima only.** The maxima of the distance between two shapes are at infinity for every unbounded pair, and a saddle answers no question a caller has asked. Shapes that meet report each crossing at distance zero; two surfaces meeting along a curve report one point of it, like any other valley.

**Closed forms first.** A whole elementary pair with a closed form is solved exactly and reports `Complete`: a line against a plane, a sphere or a cylinder, a circle against a plane, two planes, two spheres, and two parallel or coaxial cylinders. "Whole" means bounds and periods equal to the elementary shape's, so a trimmed one goes to the search, which reads its bounds. A valley of equal minima (a line parallel to a plane, concentric spheres, coaxial cylinders) reports one pair: the first shape at its reference parameters where the valley passes there.

**Every other pair is sampled, and says so.** Each operand is sampled on a coarse grid (24 samples a finite surface direction, 96 along a curve, a geometric progression along an unbounded direction, as in the curve-pair search). Every sample of one operand is paired with the nearest sample of the other, and the discrete minima of that distance seed the minimiser from both operands, so a far wall of a channel behind a nearer one is still found. The minimiser is nested: the outer parameters (the curve's, or the first surface's) take bounded Newton steps on the distance to their projection on the second surface, found from the last one, with the outer Hessian reduced by the projection's (its Schur complement), so the outer step is the joint one. The result reports `Sampled`. A proven search on Bézier pieces, as `curve_curve_pieces.rs` does for two curves, is the follow-up for the B-spline pairs.

**The same answer at any size and placement (ADR 0051).** The similarity judge covers both pairs, with no known failures. What it took:

- Along a valley the gradient is rounding alone. Rates below the rounding of the coordinates read as zero, and each outer step is held to a window: a period about the start in a periodic direction, and, in an unbounded one, as far as the seeds reach. Without it the step ran off along the valley to where the coordinates round coarser than the tolerance.
- Two minima are one basin when their distances agree within the tolerance (or the lower is a contact) and no ridge rises between them. Its way is walked on the outer shape, each projection started from the last in steps of a quarter of the scale, so it stays on one sheet of the other shape and must arrive at the other minimum's point; or, for a valley of the second surface over one outer point, along the straight way in both parameter spaces. A basin reports its lowest minimum, replaced by a representative read at the reference parameters, so that a valley's pair does not depend on where the search entered it.
- Near a shallow crossing far from the origin the gradient of the squared distance (the gap times the sine between the shapes) falls below rounding first, and the minimiser stops short. Gauss–Newton steps on the gap itself close it.
- Where a parameter collapses (a sphere's pole, a cone's apex) the box test on the parameters cannot tell a minimum, so the distance is read around the point instead. Where the outer point sits on a centre of curvature of the second surface (a cylinder's axis), the projection is not isolated and the reduced Hessian overstates the curvature; a minimum there is kept only if the distance does not fall a probe step away along each outer parameter.

**The nearest sample is a plain scan.** The grids hold a few hundred samples, and those of one operand often lie at much the same distance from the other's, where a box hierarchy pruned little: the scan halved the search's instructions on the benchmarks.

**Python.** A 3D curve's `extrema(other)` takes a surface too, whole or trimmed, and a surface's `extrema(other)` takes a curve or a surface, on every surface class and `TrimmedSurface`. A point stays an operand of the B-spline and Bézier surfaces only; the other surfaces raise `TypeError` for it and name `project_point`, as the point extrema of #247 were closed as not planned. `minimum_distance(other)` on curves and surfaces gives the closest minimum or `None`. The surfaces' `extrema` moved from the B-spline and Bézier method set `surface_extrema` into `parametric_surface`, which every surface and `TrimmedSurface` take (ADR 0029).
