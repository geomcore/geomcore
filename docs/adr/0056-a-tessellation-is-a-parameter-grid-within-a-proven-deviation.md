# A tessellation is a parameter grid within a proven deviation

`tessellate` covers a rectangle of a surface's parameters with triangles: a grid of `u` and `v` values, a vertex on the surface at each pair, each cell cut along its diagonal into two triangles. Every point of the surface over the rectangle lies within the reported deviation of the triangles, and every point of the triangles within it of the surface. The grid's spacing is planned from a bound before any vertex is computed, as a curve's discretization is (ADR 0003), and the vertices, normals, `(u, v)` pairs and triangles go into buffers the caller owns, counted first (ADR 0004).

The bound comes from what each family offers in closed form:

- **A plane** is one cell, deviation zero.
- **A cylinder, cone, sphere and torus** turn a straight or circular meridian about their axis. A cell's four vertices form a trapezoid in a plane, which its triangles cover exactly; the surface over the cell lies within `ρ s(h_u) + δ` of it both ways, `ρ` the cell's larger distance from the axis, `s(h) = 1 − cos(h/2)` the sagitta of a unit circle's arc of angle `h`, and `δ` the meridian's own chordal deviation (zero for a line, `r s(h_v)` for a circle). That needs the lemma that an arc and its chord, both run at constant speed, are never farther apart than the sagitta, which holds for arcs up to a half turn; steps are at most a third of a turn. Half the deviation goes to the meridian and the rest to the turn, which for products of two terms like these gives the fewest cells.
- **A surface of revolution** takes its profile's discretization (within half the deviation) as the meridian, and the same bound plus the cell's twist: a profile out of the planes through the axis twists each quad by `2 sin(h_u/2)` times its chord's part turning about the axis, and the triangles then lie within that twist's part across the rows of the bilinear patch. A chord crossing the axis gives a crossed quad, bounded by a quarter of the whole twist instead.
- **An extrusion** takes its profile's discretization across and one step along the sweep: each cell is the profile's chord swept, a parallelogram, and the chord deviation bounds the distance both ways.
- **A B-spline or Bézier surface** takes equal steps in each knot span. On a cell of steps `h_u` and `h_v` the linear interpolation deviates from a polynomial patch by at most `(M_uu h_u² + 2 M_uv h_u h_v + M_vv h_v²) / 8`, the `M` bounding its second partials from the second differences of its Bézier control points; a rational patch works on its homogeneous control points as a rational curve does. The cross term is split between the two directions, and each span's count is the largest its patches need.

The deviation is reported with whether it is proven. It is, but on a swept surface whose profile is a quartic branch, whose discretization measures its deviation on samples (ADR 0003 allowed that one curve to be sampled); the result then says it was estimated rather than claiming a bound.

Where a row of the grid lies within `tol.confusion` of a point (a sphere's pole, a cone's apex, a B-spline boundary whose control points coincide), its vertices are made that one point, the triangles left without area are not written, and the plan pays for the distance any vertex moved out of the deviation before it sets the spacing. A cone's apex inside the range splits the grid there into two rows of vertices at the same point, one for each nappe, since the normals of the two nappes along a meridian are opposite. A normal the partials do not give is the surface's own recovery (ADR 0010), else the normal halfway into the vertex's cells. A range spanning a whole period gives a last row or column at exactly the first one's points, with its own parameters, so the triangles close up and texture coordinates do not jump.

The tolerance is an argument, unlike a curve's discretization: it decides which rows collapse and gives the normals, as everywhere else. The range is part of the criteria: an unbounded direction needs one, a bounded one defaults to its bounds, and a periodic one may cross its seam. Triangle indices are `u32`, what graphics buffers and mesh formats take; a tessellation needing more vertices is refused before anything is allocated. Planning allocates the grid's parameters, a few numbers per row and column, so `tessellate_into` is not free of allocation as `discretize_into` is, but it never allocates per vertex. This amends ADR 0003 and ADR 0004 for surfaces.

## Considered options

- **A grid refined cell by cell** (a quadtree, or splitting where the bound is largest): rejected for now. Neighbouring cells of different sizes leave cracks unless the coarse side is cut to match, which costs more code than it saves triangles on the surfaces of the tests. A tensor grid is watertight by construction, and on a B-spline its spacing already varies span by span.
- **Measuring the deviation on samples** (the middle of each triangle against the surface), as many tessellators do for display: rejected for the reason ADR 0003 rejected it for curves. Metrology and the classification of points against faces need a bound, and the a priori plan costs a few operations per row.
- **Converting every surface to B-spline patches** (ADR 0026) and using the spline bound: rejected. The exact rational patches of a circle have a parameter that is not the angle, so the vertices' `(u, v)` would have to be mapped back, and the spline bound on a rational patch is looser than the sagitta.
- **Splitting the profile of a twisted surface of revolution finer to share its twist**: tried and left out. The twist grows with both steps, so the vertex count it costs is the same however the steps are shared, and the extra rows only added vertices on the cases measured.
- **The deviation at the same parameters only** (the triangle's linear interpolation against the surface at the interpolated `(u, v)`): not the reported quantity. A planar trapezoid's triangles cover its bilinear patch exactly but at other parameters, so that measure would double the triangles of every elementary surface for nothing a caller can see. The reported deviation bounds the distance between the point sets, both ways.
- **Normals of the triangles, or none**: rejected. Shading and offsetting need the surface's normal at the vertex, which the surface gives exactly.
